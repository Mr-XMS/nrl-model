Method

This page describes what the model does and what it does not do. It is written to be checked. Every parameter below is the value actually used in the code that produced the forecasts in the archive.

The weekly clock

The forecast is produced on Tuesday because that is when team lists are published — the single largest piece of information in the week. It is frozen at that moment and committed. Nothing is revised afterwards.

Monday
Results scraped, last round graded, player statistics refreshed.
Tuesday
Team lists out. The forecast is computed, frozen and committed. Subscribers see it now.
Thu – Sun
Prices and late lineup changes are logged, but the recorded forecast does not change.
Sunday midnight
The round opens free to everyone, here, in full.

The unlock is a clock event. It does not wait on results being scraped or games being marked complete, so an outage can delay the scoreline column but never the forecast itself.

Six layers

Each layer adjusts the probability produced by the one above it. The order matters: team quality first, then who is playing, then how well they play, then whether they are fit, then the conditions, then the market.

1

Team rating

Every club carries an Elo rating updated after each result. Beating a stronger team moves the rating more than beating a weaker one, and ratings regress toward the mean between seasons so a premiership does not follow a squad forever.

start 1500 · K = 32 · home advantage = 55 pts
between-season regression = 30% · trained on 3,585 matches since 2009
2

Who is actually playing

Team lists are published on Tuesday. The model reads the named 17, measures how much of last week’s side has been retained, and tracks how long the spine has played together — the positions that carry a side’s structure.

spine = Fullback, Five-eighth, Halfback, Hooker
retention and spine continuity computed per side, per round
3

Player quality from statistics

Each player carries a rating built from their per-game career statistics rather than reputation. Players with few games are shrunk toward the league average, so a debutant is treated as average rather than as whatever their first two games happened to look like.

shrinkage prior = 8 games
ratings refresh weekly as new match statistics land
4

Availability

The NRL casualty ward is scraped each week. Players returning from injury are discounted for rust; players carrying a doubt are discounted harder, because the named side is not always the side that runs out.

returning from injury × 0.80
listed in doubt × 0.60
5

Conditions

The rain forecast for each venue city is pulled at prediction time. Wet games historically level the contest: favourites win less often than their rating says, and totals run lower. The size of that effect was learned from the historical record, not asserted.

wet threshold = 5mm forecast rainfall
effect fitted across all historical matches with match-day rainfall
6

The market

The bookmaker price is de-vigged and averaged with the model on the log-odds scale. This is deliberate: the market aggregates information the model cannot see, and the model sees lineup detail the market sometimes prices slowly. Neither is trusted alone.

blend = 50/50 logit average of model and de-vigged price

Concurrent scenarios

Two different things run in parallel on every fixture, for two different reasons.

Competing versions of the model

Five versions forecast every match at the same moment. Only version A is published as the forecast; all five are frozen and graded. The point is that arguments about whether an adjustment helps get settled by the record instead of by opinion — and an idea that fails gets removed.

A — baseline
The full model with no experimental adjustment. This is the number published as the forecast.
B — fitness ramp
Identical to A, except returning players are discounted on a graded ramp rather than a flat multiplier.
C — weather
Identical to A, plus a leveller that shrinks short-priced favourites when heavy rain is forecast at the venue.
Market
The bookmaker price with the margin removed. Not a model — the opponent every model is measured against.
Blend
A 50/50 logit average of A and the market. If the model carries information the market lacks, this should beat both.

Live 2026 standings across 31 graded matches. Lower log loss is better; it rewards being confident and right and punishes being confident and wrong, which raw accuracy does not. This sample is far too small to conclude anything. Separating these versions reliably needs hundreds of matches, not dozens, and the differences between A, B and C are currently smaller than the noise. The table is published because watching a verdict fail to form is part of the record.

Lineup scenarios

When a player is flagged as returning or in doubt, the fixture is forecast three times over — once assuming they are fully fit, once as the team sheet names them, once assuming they are withdrawn.

Declared fit
South-Sydney-Rabbitohs win 62.7%
As named
South-Sydney-Rabbitohs win 62.5%
Withdrawn
South-Sydney-Rabbitohs win 61.2%

Worked example from the current round: David Fifita (South-Sydney-Rabbitohs, returning from injury). The three worlds are computed together and the spread between them — here 1.5 points — is the honest measure of how much this one selection actually matters. Most players move the number by less than a point. Publishing the spread stops a late withdrawal being retrofitted into an excuse.

What the record says so far

Across twelve walk-forward seasons — each one predicted using only the seasons before it — the team model calls about 65% of matches, with season-to-season swings between roughly 58% and 74%. Tested against exchange closing prices on 609 matches, the full model is close to level with the market on log loss, and a blend of the two scored better than either alone.

That last result is the reason this project exists, and it is also the one most likely to be wrong. It was measured once, on historical data, with a blend weight chosen while looking at that same data. The 2026 season is the first live test of it, and so far the market is ahead. Whether that gap closes or widens is the actual research question, and the archive is how it gets answered in public.

Known weaknesses

Stated plainly, because a forecast you cannot criticise is not worth reading.

  • Several parameters are set by hand, not fitted. The Elo K factor, the home advantage, the injury multipliers and the shrinkage prior are reasonable values rather than optimised ones. Home advantage in particular is treated as identical at every venue, which is certainly wrong.
  • The wet-weather flag is a hard threshold. 5mm forecast counts as wet and 4.9mm does not, which is a crude way to model rain and has already produced at least one game that fell the wrong side of the line.
  • Team-level surges are missed. Individual returning players are discounted, but nothing captures a spine reassembling all at once. The market prices this and the model has been caught by it.
  • The largest disagreements with the market have been the worst calls. Historically big divergences carried more signal; this season they have not. If that holds, the model should shrink toward the market harder as disagreement grows.
  • Margins are discarded. The model predicts who wins, not by how much, which throws away most of the information in a result.
  • Features were selected against one test window. Their measured gains are optimistic, and some will shrink under proper testing.

What this is not

It is not betting advice and there is no system here for beating a bookmaker. Roughly a third of the calls in the archive are wrong, and there is no arrangement of any kind with any wagering operator. It is a forecasting project published in advance, because a prediction made after the fact is not a prediction.
